Frequently asked questions

No referral is needed. You can contact us directly to book an appointment. Podiatrists are primary care practitioners and you do not need a GP letter. If we identify anything that requires GP attention during your visit, we will advise you accordingly.
Clean feet and a comfortable chair or bed to sit in with your feet raised or accessible is all you need. If you take any medication relevant to foot health (such as blood thinners or diabetes medication), it is helpful to have a list of these ready for the first visit. Socks can stay on until we arrive.
Most routine appointments take around 30 minutes. Diabetic assessments and some clinical treatments such as ingrown toenail work take closer to 45 minutes. We will always tell you the expected duration when confirming your booking.
Yes, and we particularly encourage diabetic patients to use a regular podiatry service. Our diabetic foot assessment is specifically designed to detect early changes in circulation and sensation, reducing the risk of complications. Regular reviews are recommended at least annually for all diabetic patients, and more often for those with additional risk factors.
Absolutely. Many of our appointments are arranged by family members for elderly parents or relatives. When you contact us, just let us know that you are enquiring on behalf of someone else and provide the patient's address, along with any relevant information about their health or mobility.
Payment is made at the end of each appointment. We accept cash and bank transfer. Payment details will be confirmed when booking.
